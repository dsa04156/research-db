## 요약·초록

The article argues that traditional SaaS and context-aware systems cannot serve domain-speci c needs (Sections 1.1 and 1.2), that "Vertical AI agents" close that gap (Section 2), and that such agents are built from four modules -Memory, Reasoning Engine, Cognitive Skills, Tools (Sections 3.3.1 through 3.3.4)ofwhich Cognitive Skills is claimed as the author's own contribution (Abstract; Section 3.1: "We introduce a new module to the core building blocks of the LLM agent").Section 4 then sorts agentic systems into three categories -Task-Speci c, Multi-Agent, Human-Augmented (Section 4.3) -and walks through one reference pattern for each: the RAG Agent Router (Figure 3), the RAG Orchestrated Multi-Agent System (Figure 4), and the HITL Agent (Figure 5).Everything that holds this up is de nitional and illustrative.The article contains no data, no experiment, no benchmark, no deployment record, and no comparison table; the support is a stated architecture plus 14 references, 6 of which (references 4 through 9) are vendor blog posts and product pages.As a teaching map of the current design vocabulary, it reads well, and the gures are sensibly chosen.As a review article in the scholarly sense -a survey with a method, a scope, and an evidentiary standard -it is not yet there, and that is the gap this report is mostly about. Does the claim hold on the dataThere is no data, which is legitimate for a review, so the question becomes whether the claims are of a kind a review can make without any.Several are not.Section 2.1.1 asserts that vertical agents perform "legal contract analysis, medical imaging interpretation, or nancial risk assessment, with exceptional precision"; Section 2.1.3asserts "Faster Turnaround Times: Analyzing, deciding, and executing tasks Qeios qeios.

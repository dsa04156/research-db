## 요약·초록

The tool calls, intermediate outputs, artifacts, and hashes generated during execution may not match the fluent step-level accounts generated by tool-using agents.This challenge is examined in this study as tool-use claim faithfulness: a claimed step is faithful only if it is supported by both the expected evidence relation over deterministic outputs and a matching execution event.The gap is diagnostic: final-task success can indicate whether a result was achieved, but it cannot determine whether the stated procedure was followed, which step diverged first, or whether the failure was caused by a wrong tool, wrong argument, missing call, or broken evidence relation.For brief deterministic agentic workflows, we present VerifierBench as a controlled trace-evidence evaluation framework and procedural faithfulness audit.Faithful controls attain ETR=CTR=TCV=1.000 and E2E=1.000 in MicroBench-300, which covers 300 tasks, 1,497 expected steps, three deterministic tools, four agent variants, and controlled E2/E3 perturbations.The essential difference between procedural faithfulness and final-task success is revealed by a step-skipping baseline that yields ETR=0.743but E2E=0.143.While wrong-argument perturbation results in ETR=0.683 and E2E=0.103,wrong-tool perturbation produces ETR=0.940 and E2E=0.700.The findings demonstrate that under controlled deterministic conditions, trace-evidence verification can reveal, categorize, and pinpoint tool-use hallucinations.
